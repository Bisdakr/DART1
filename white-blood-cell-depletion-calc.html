Goal here is maximal WBC removal per session, not preservation of a clean cellular product. Different from HPC-A — settings shift accordingly.
Collection Preference (instrument color tool)
Where it lives: the instrument's Collection Preference Tool — printed color strip from dark red/brown (denser, more cells in product) to light peach (more plasma, fewer cells).
For WBC depletion: a moderate-to-slightly-darker setting captures more white cells per pass. Going too dark removes too much RBC and can drop patient Hct.
How to adjust: only after the message bar reads "Collecting MNC". Increment by 10, wait ≥ 2 min, then assess collect-line color.
Watch product Hct: if product Hct climbs > 5–6%, you're losing too many RBCs — pull back lighter.
Verify with your institutional protocol — collection-preference targets for therapeutic depletion vary by center.
Inlet Flow & Alarm Discipline
Steady > fast. Slower with no alarms removes more WBC than fast with alarms — every alarm pause halts the collection layer.
Hyperleukocytosis blood is viscous. Expect higher inlet pressures; consider lower starting flow and titrate up.
Access: central catheter strongly preferred for therapeutic depletion. Femoral often necessary for emergent leukostasis cases.
Anticoagulation Balance
Standard: ACD-A 12:1.
Hyperleukocytosis caveat: very high WBC + clumping risk → may need richer ratio (10:1 or 8:1). Watch for citrate symptoms in renal failure / pediatric patients.
Heparin add-on (per institutional protocol) sometimes used to reduce clumping in extreme hyperleukocytosis.
Citrate & Calcium Strategy
Calcium Gluconate per the calculator (1.5–2 g per 4000 mL processed) — continuous infusion or scheduled boluses; peripheral or central line acceptable.
Pre-procedure calcium load (oral or IV) blunts symptom onset.
Renal failure / oliguria: citrate clears more slowly — expect more symptoms; use ionized Ca trends.
Patient Monitoring
Q15-min vitals for first hour, then per protocol. Continuous SpO₂ and telemetry for severe hyperleukocytosis (leukostasis risk persists during run).
Neuro checks at start, midpoint, and end — leukostasis can deteriorate during procedure if delayed.
Watch for TLS: hyperK, hyperPO₄, hyperuric, hypoCa — coordinate with rasburicase / IV fluids per oncology team.
Replacement & Post-Procedure
Replacement fluid: typically NS or LR; albumin (5%) if removal > 15% TBV.
Post-procedure CBC within 1–4 h to confirm WBC drop and trend platelets.
Daily reassessment — repeat leukapheresis if WBC remains > 100 ×10³/µL or symptoms persist.
Cytoreductive chemo (hydroxyurea 50–100 mg/kg/day) and induction should NOT be delayed for apheresis.
